## 1. The finding: all six targets are competitors, and one is not a roundup

D3's rationale is *"getting added to a ranking roundup is far cheaper than outranking it."* That is
true of **independent** roundups. Every one of the six named is published by a company selling a
competing QR generator, and each ranks its own product first.

Verified 2026-08-03 by reading the pages:

| Target | Runs its own QR generator? | Ranks itself #1? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jotform** | Yes | Yes | Won't add a rival |
| **ME-QR** | Yes — it *is* a QR company | Yes | Won't add a rival |
| **Andrew Twelftree** | Yes | **Not a roundup at all** | Nothing to be added to |
| **MakeBranded** | Yes | Yes ("admin" byline, no email) | Won't add a rival |
| **QRForever** | Yes | Yes | Won't add a rival |
| **Fotify** | Yes | Yes | Won't add a rival |

The Andrew Twelftree one is worth stating plainly: it is **his own competing tool**, listing no
alternatives at all. There is no list to join. §1 of the plan counted it as one of "6 roundups";
that was wrong.

**So the honest expected yield on the six as specified is close to zero.** Asking a company to add
a free, no-signup competitor above or beside its own paid funnel is not a cold-email problem, it is
a conflict-of-interest problem.

**This does not kill D3 — it retargets it.** The roundups worth pitching are published by companies
in *adjacent* categories, who have no QR product to protect and do recommend third-party tools.

## 3. The pitch

The angle from the plan still holds and is genuinely differentiated: **free, no sign-up,
non-expiring, and client-side** — and per the plan and the AEO playbook, **be honest that codes are
static.** Stated limitations get cited; overselling gets discarded.

## 4. Rules

- **One email each, no follow-up chasing.** A second email to someone who ignored the first
  converts poorly and costs reputation you will want later.
- **Never claim to be better than the tool they own.** They will read it, and it ends the thread.
- **Always state the static-only limitation.** Both because it is true and because a reviewer who
  finds an unstated catch drops you permanently.
- **Do not offer a link exchange, payment or a guest post.** It reframes you as a link buyer, and
  several of these publish sponsored placements — that is a different, paid conversation and the
  plan rules out paid acquisition.
- **Track what you send** in §5, so a future session does not re-pitch the same person.

## 6. Honest expectation, and when to stop

Realistically: **one or two placements out of ten sends is a good result**, and the plan's 4-hour
estimate buys maybe ten well-researched sends. Even a success here is a single referring link — it
does not move the money query on its own.

Given the D1 and E4 findings (the site is indexed cleanly but absent from every answer surface, and
the brand name is shared by at least six entities), **D1 and D2 are better uses of the same hours**:
directory listings are durable and LLM-cited, and a launch produces a burst of independent mentions
rather than one negotiated link.

**Suggested order: D1 → D2 → D3**, and treat D3 as the optional tail. If the first five sends get
no reply, stop — that is the signal, and continuing is sunk cost.
